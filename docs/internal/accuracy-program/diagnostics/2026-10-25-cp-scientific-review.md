## What the probe establishes

The extracted Rust function matches source verbatim (`six_dof.rs:1223–1242`; **probe** `scripts/diagnostics/engine-cp-convergence.mjs:40–72`). Fallback differences in the separate TS preparation are immaterial here because retained contributions have finite CPs. The JSON records maximum TS CP disagreement `4.44e-16 m`, CNα disagreement `7.11e-15`, and moment-identity residual `2.05e-12 N m`. This verifies arithmetic/reconstruction consistency, not aerodynamic truth or a production flight telemetry channel.
- **The scalar CP comparisons have compatible geometry, station units and Mach.** Both stations are meters aft of the nose. The curve takes no alpha argument (`aero.rs:1927–1932,1950–1955`); the flight scalar also takes only Mach and prepared geometry. Therefore the alpha sweep checks how the implemented force law applies that station; it does not measure CP versus alpha. Nominal curve-minus-flight differences are +0.023781 m at M0.8, +0.102295 m at M1.4, +0.163240 m at M2 and +0.555462 m at M4. They are numerical model disagreements, not measured physical errors.
- **Do not substitute margins or infer flight outcomes.** Curve margins use dry component CG (`aero.rs:2268`); loads use dry mass plus remaining motor mass at its modeled centroid (`six_dof.rs:1296–1315`). The probe compares CP directly and uses the same combined CG for both moment calculations, so that mismatch does not cause its CP deltas. Its conditions are coasting at 3.82 s, ISA sea level, no wind/cant/recovery, identity attitude and zero angular rates. These are controlled local states, not necessarily reachable points on a trajectory. M0 has zero aerodynamic load and undefined flow incidence; its CP is a model coefficient/limit, not an observable center of force. At alpha zero and positive speed, static moment is zero but its alpha derivative need not be.
- **Reference diameter matters for the synthetic comparison.** The nominal diameter is 0.152 m; the modified vehicle uses 0.120 m. The boattail's M0 disagreement is +0.056090 m / 0.4674 calibers within that same modified vehicle. Changes in caliber counts across the two vehicles also reflect this denominator change. The synthetic vehicle is a signed-transition diagnostic, not a validated airframe.

## Why the paths disagree

**Mach treatment:** curve CP reweights a selected first trapezoid/freeform fin contribution with a swept-normal-Mach finite-wing expression, whose ratio is floored at 1 (`aero.rs:1577–1596,1961–1976,2079–2124,2149–2154`). It then subtracts a separate forward shift explicitly labeled unvalidated in sign and magnitude (`:1981–1988`). Flight instead reweights all prepared fins with `min(1,1/sqrt(M²−1))` and retains the body term (`six_dof.rs:1223–1241`). Its degradation therefore starts only above **M=√2**, not immediately above M1. The curve can enhance fin weighting while flight leaves it constant; at high Mach the curve floor preserves its baseline fin weighting while flight reduces it. The curve's special ARCAS rake geometry correction (`aero.rs:1522–1559`) is another possible path difference, but does not explain the nominal NASA preset's observed differences. Multi-fin and elliptical-fin configurations are not covered by this two-fixture probe.

Named classical sources for the curve ingredients do not establish that the composite formula, sonic treatment, floor and independent CP shift form a qualified rocket normal-force model. Nor does flight's internally consistent reweighting establish its physical accuracy. Both functions cover overlapping Mach conditions; there is no source-grounded distinction here such as one being a measured finite-alpha CP and the other a small-angle derivative CP.

**Signed contribution loss:** the transition formula deliberately yields negative CNα for a contraction (`aero.rs:994–1016`), and stability sums signed force slopes and signed first moments (`:1389–1397`). Flight preparation drops that term (`models.rs:475–477`). In the synthetic vehicle the omitted term is CNα = −1.208888889 at x = 1.457647059 m. This alone accounts for its low-speed disagreement; dropping a zero term in the nominal fixture does not. This is an implementation inconsistency relative to the adopted method, even without new tunnel measurements. Negative component CNα is not intrinsically invalid.

## Moment convention and an unresolved coefficient definition

For the probe's positive-alpha pitch plane, `v_B=(0,V cos α,V sin α)`, define `N=qS CNα sin α` and `D=qS CD`. Source gives `F_Bz=−N−D sin α`, with `r_CP−r_CG=(0,−(x_CP−x_CG),0)`. Thus

`M_Bx=(x_CP−x_CG)(N+D sin α)`.

This matches the probe's sign and drag projection (`six_dof.rs:1422–1434,1445–1474`; **probe** `:81–90`). CP aft of CG gives the restoring sign under these axes. “drag_axial” is actually the wind-axis drag magnitude, not a body-axis axial coefficient. At fixed Mach the model's total body-transverse small-angle slope is **CNα + CD**, while its supplied normal-force slope alone is CNα. Dividing static moment by N alone would therefore produce an apparent arm multiplied by `1+CD/CNα`; dividing by total transverse force recovers the implemented arm. Neither division is meaningful when its denominator vanishes. A finite-alpha force CP and an alpha-derivative CP must also be distinguished in future evidence comparisons.

Gravity acts through combined CG and contributes no modeled moment; thrust is axial with no modeled offset torque. Zero rates eliminate pitch/yaw/roll damping, and zero cant eliminates roll torque. For general states, damping must be removed before inferring static CP; recovery sets the static arm to zero, and rail constraints/tail-first rules change interpretation (`six_dof.rs:1372–1420,1438–1474`). Inertia and inertia-rate terms govern rotational acceleration, not this aerodynamic CP identity.

There remains a **force-definition question**: is the Barrowman CNα intended as total body-normal force, or an additional contribution alongside wind-axis drag? If it denotes total body-normal force, adding the lateral drag projection changes that defined coefficient. If it denotes an additional contribution, its scope and the assumption that drag shares its moment station require support. The existing code/TS agreement settles implementation behavior only. Do not remove drag projection or change force axes merely to make CP numbers agree. The probe's CP-only substitution keeps flight CNα, drag and CG fixed; it is a mechanical sensitivity calculation, not a self-consistent alternative model prediction.

## Concrete signed-contribution follow-up specification

**Method basis already in the repository:** `src/aero/barrowman.ts:1–4` cites James S. Barrowman, *The Practical Calculation of the Aerodynamic Characteristics of Slender Finned Vehicles*, NASA TM X-67216. Its normal slope is explicitly a per-radian derivative (`:24`); the signed transition equation is at `:52–64`, mirrored in Rust `aero.rs:994–1016`. Together with linear force/moment superposition, this is sufficient to justify preserving negative component contributions in the existing method. This review has not independently re-read the primary report and does not extend its applicability to transonic/supersonic flow or validate the repository's interference approximations.

**Scope and representation.** Replace the lossy prepared `(bodySlope, bodyCP, finSlope, finCP)` representation with `(B, J_B, F, J_F)`, where each group holds `Σ CNα_i` and `Σ CNα_i x_i` respectively. Retain finite signed nonzero terms and require a finite station for every nonzero contribution; do not silently convert invalid values to zero or a CG station. Zero-CNα terms contribute zero moment. At each Mach retain the current flight effectiveness factor `g(M)` unchanged and form

`C(M) = B + g(M) F; J(M) = J_B + g(M) J_F; CP(M) = J(M)/C(M)`.

A zero or negative **group** slope is allowed and must not discard its first moment. No group CP division is needed. Translation to a station `x_ref` must obey `J_ref = J − x_ref C`. Within well-conditioned positive-total states, retain the existing full-vector drag and moment assembly; changing their definition is a separate model task. Do not import the curve's lift factor, CP shift, or planform corrections into this repair.

**Fail closed.** For the present CP-times-total-lateral-force formulation, nonfinite `C`/`J`, near-zero total `C`, or netnegative total `C` must yield an explicit unsupported/error result, not a fallback CP, a “stable” margin, or a valid continuation with zero moment. This is a conservative support boundary for the present formulation, not a statement that negative net normal slope is physically impossible. A defensible initial near-zero floor is the existing stability denominator floor `1e-4` (`aero.rs:1393`), explicitly named as an inherited numerical/model-support policy in per-radian coefficient units, not a measured physical threshold. Also guard numerical cancellation relative to the sum of absolute contributing slopes and reject nonfinite derived CP. The implementation should document the exact cancellation bound and boundary comparison, test both sides, and avoid broad changes to the separate stability/curve API. A future force-plus-free-couple model could support zero/negative totals without dividing, but current drag-at-CP usage prevents treating that as a trivial extension. Propagate failure before using the undefined arm; merely marking an already-NaN result unsupported is insufficient.
