# Concrete model replacement path — 2026-10-25

Decision: build one source-defined force-and-moment evaluator, then qualify a declared collegiate vehicle family with measured inputs and independent flights. The first substantive change should resolve force definitions and carry independent moments; replacing the CP curve alone cannot do this. Atmosphere/Reynolds, measured mass properties, and recovery data are separate, practical contributors to useful accuracy. Transonic accuracy is attainable through qualified supplied coefficient data or a reviewed engineering model plus measurements; it is not established by a smooth interpolation or by CFD alone.

Source baseline: `761d6c3fd80ba7188e00603dee741719ef1a41c5`, `/workspace/astraea`, read only. This is planning and source analysis, with no new simulation, build, kernel edit, raw-flight inspection, holdout exposure, or numeric accuracy threshold. Existing matched-CP, signed-repair, calibration and output-contract reviews were read. The signed repair, event-qualified refit and recorded-peak retention are completed foundations, not work proposed again. New statements below distinguish exact algebra from physical hypotheses. Acceptance budgets must be fixed from the existing proposed mission/decision specification before scoring.

## 1. Exact remaining force-definition problem: fix before selecting a Mach law

**Current code.** `crates/astraea-core/src/six_dof.rs:1236` preserves signed slopes/first moments; `:1485–1500` adds a body-transverse normal force to a vector of wind-axis drag; `:1509–1538` applies the normal CP arm to the entire transverse aerodynamic force. `FlightLoadsDiagnostic` at `:1285` now accurately labels its input slope as an *added* normal contribution. That label documents the implementation; it does not make the coefficient the body-normal derivative used by Barrowman.

**Exact consequence, derived from the inspected code.** In the pitch plane take positive incidence with `v_B=(0,V cos α,V sin α)`, `C=CNα_input`, and `D0=CD_table`. At fixed Mach, zero rates/cant and no recovery/rail:

```
F_y/(qS) = −D0 cos α
F_z/(qS) = −(C+D0) sin α
CN_total = (C+D0) sin α
CA_total = D0 cos α
CD_total = −F·v/(qS V) = D0 + C sin² α
CL_total = C sin α cos α
M_x/(qS) = (xCP−xCG)(C+D0) sin α
```

Thus total body-normal derivative is `C+D0`, not `C`; the reported explicit drag term is not the drag of the complete vector at finite incidence. The NASA D-4013 coefficient definitions use **body-axis normal/axial force and an independently measured pitching moment**, about a stated station. They cannot be compared directly to the present added-normal input. [NASA TN D-4013, symbols and measurement definitions, printed pp2–4](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19670020050/downloads/19670020050.pdf).

**Concrete candidate.** Evaluate total body `CN`, `CA` and a static moment about a fixed datum. Preserve `CD0=CA0` at zero incidence. Translate the moment to instantaneous CG; derive the wind-axis output by an exact rotation. In this plane the identities are `CD=CA cosα+CN sinα`, `CL=CN cosα−CA sinα`. This is compatible with normative contract §6 because the body coefficients are explicitly identified and converted. Generalize with the contract's paired alpha/beta definitions and its +Y-forward frame. Do not independently add wind drag to the total body-normal vector.

The first bounded physical candidate can use the existing signed small-angle normal model and `M_x=qS(J−xCG C)α`, with axial `CA0` as a **leading-order small-incidence approximation**. Alternatively retain a prescribed total `CD(α)` and solve `CA=(CD−CN sinα)/cosα` in its supported forward-flight domain. These agree at zero incidence but make different finite-incidence assumptions. Select the finite-incidence axial extension through the source/matched-coefficient experiment below; neither is an algebraically compelled universal drag law. Do not treat `sinα` alone as a validated 30-degree extension.

**Bounded experiment and decisive identities.** Extend the existing native local-load evaluator, preserving a legacy model ID, to accept the new coefficient packet. For signed pitch/yaw incidence, zero incidence, datum translation, negative group slopes and cancellation, verify vector rotation, `F·v`, `M_CG=M_datum+(r_datum−r_CG)×F`, and the alpha derivatives. In particular vary only `CA0`: the declared body-normal derivative and its specified static moment must remain fixed. Vary only CG: force remains fixed and moment changes by the cross-product identity. Test zero normal force with a finite free couple without dividing by CP. These establish semantics without flight/tunnel data.

**Acceptance/fallback.** Independent frame/mechanics review must verify the equations and actual Rust/WASM diagnostic behavior, then quantify before/after consequences on the same four exposed development inputs with every fit/motor setting frozen. No apogee-error improvement criterion. Keep legacy projects bound to their original model ID until explicit migration. If finite-incidence evidence is insufficient, retain a declared limited approximation/UNKNOWN for the affected decision; do not quietly revert to CP-times-total-force or invent a qualified angle limit.

## 2. Mach-dependent normal force and moment: replace the pair, not one CP scalar

**Current code.** Flight `normal_slope_at_mach` uses `g=min(1,1/sqrt(M²−1))` above M1, so fin degradation starts only above √2; body weighting is constant. Curve `aero.rs:1577–1596,1950–1990` uses a swept-normal-Mach finite-wing ratio floored at one, reweights one selected fin set, then applies an explicitly unsourced forward shift and minimum station. Special ARCAS dimensions trigger an implicit rake correction (`:1522–1559`). Flight includes all prepared fin groups. The prior matched probe quantified the disagreement; signed repair did not select either Mach law. Neither deserves production authority solely because another implementation reproduces it.

**Published candidates and actual scope.** Barrowman's 1967 thesis explicitly covers slender axisymmetric bodies with three/four fins, with separate subsonic and supersonic methods and no transonic derivation. Its synopsis/symbol definitions were inspected, not every equation. [NASA primary thesis](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20010047838/downloads/20010047838.pdf). Niskanen's actual method document supplies a more directly implementable reference: small-angle component assembly, compressible finite-fin slope, supersonic strip approximation, fin-CP interpolation and body-crossflow extension. Its subsonic emphasis and approximations must be retained in the model's claim. [OpenRocket 13.05 technical documentation, §§3.1–3.4](https://openrocket.sourceforge.net/techdoc.pdf).

**Recommended production candidate order.** First implement/review the complete small-angle subsonic coefficient pair for ordinary measured three/four-fin geometry, including each fin set and signed body terms. Next add a separately identified supersonic branch using published finite-fin/strip and body methods with their geometry restrictions. Treat a transonic bridge as an empirical/model-discrepancy problem, not as a consequence of Prandtl–Glauert/Ackeret formulas. An interpolation must operate on `CN`/moment or their derivatives, never just a CP ratio. Include a supplied-coefficient-table provider in the same interface so an otherwise unsupported collegiate mission can use independently qualified configuration-specific data. Keep source equations, source domains and any adaptations auditable by component.

**Implementable experiment.** Freeze geometry, alpha sign convention, reference area/length, Reynolds, roll, cant, support/base correction and moment datum. Reuse the exposed ARCAS material and its original reports to test `CN(α)` and `Cm(α)` together at actual source conditions; have two reviewers independently extract/check a bounded predeclared set of relevant coefficient plots, with digitization uncertainty. Existing CP-only digitization is insufficient. Use fins-off/on and long/short configurations to separate body, fin/interference and moment errors. Evaluate small-angle derivatives and finite-incidence curves separately. Add multi-fin/elliptical/freeform analytic consistency fixtures, but keep unsupported source geometries outside the claimed model family. No fresh protected experimental set is needed for development.

D-4013 specifies M0.60–1.20, Re/m≈9.8e6, several roll/cant configurations, roughness trips and different axial-force/base corrections; its finite-alpha behavior prevents a blanket linear-stability claim. These conditions bound that evidence, not the whole collegiate fleet. The existing D-4014 reference offers a supersonic extension to investigate; its complete PDF could not be re-fetched in this session, and no newly digitized supersonic outcome is claimed.

**Acceptance/fallback.** Require matching `CNα` and moment derivative, with uncertainty and decision effect, not a visually plausible CP or improved apogee. CP becomes a derived optional ratio; unstable stiffness and undefined CP remain distinct. Publish qualified slices for actually covered geometry/conditions. If no candidate resolves transonic stability within the team's reserve, use qualified supplied data or keep that stability decision unresolved. Do not relabel all collegiate missions subsonic merely to obtain a pass.

## 3. Drag/Reynolds/power-on: remove hidden environmental and geometry assumptions

**Current code.** `models.rs:454–456,539–540` prepares two 25-point Mach-only tables. `aero.rs:2203–2215` evaluates their friction at sea-level conditions and hardcoded 5 μm roughness. Loads update density and sound speed but not that friction Reynolds number (`six_dof.rs:1358–1360,1410`). Table interpolation (`:1206`) clamps above M4. `aero.rs:2249` sets protuberance drag to zero; total Cd is floored at 0.15. Base drag (`:1798–1839`) explicitly labels its shape constants and universal 0.38 powered factor as unvalidated project fits. The current nose/fin wave terms also require equation-level applicability review. These omissions can be absorbed spuriously by a coast multiplier, obscuring the powered-flight error.

**Replacement and bounded experiment.** Split zero-incidence drag into independently inspectable surface friction, nose/fin pressure, base and actual protuberance contributions. Supply local `M,ρ,T,μ` or Re per surface, measured surface specification and actual fin outlines to the evaluator; use explicit dimensional roughness uncertainty. Make the ARCAS polygon a geometry input, removing the dimension-triggered special case. Freeze the current pressure/base formulas initially while a separate packet replaces each supported component with its fully cited candidate. Test native friction at identical Mach with two declared atmospheric/Re states and ensure only intended terms respond. Then assess the published power-off zero-incidence component evidence at its actual base/sting convention. Compare table interpolation against direct evaluation; choose resolution from coefficient/trajectory error allocation, not an arbitrary denser grid.

**Decisive evidence.** Zero-alpha measurements isolate drag from the normal-force transformation issue. For a flown article, an event-qualified, instrumented coast with measured mass and air-relative motion constrains **total coast drag**, not uniquely skin friction/base drag. Powered base drag additionally needs plume/nozzle conditions or a justified bounded model. No cold-flow tunnel or post-burn fit identifies the plume factor. If nozzle exit area and thrust reference ambient pressure are known, a separately reviewed pressure-thrust correction can be defined; the present motor record carries neither and thrust is queried without ambient pressure. Do not add a second correction to an already altitude-corrected curve.

**Acceptance/fallback.** Accept analytic Reynolds/area scaling and interpolation tests as implementation checks. Accept physical drag only where independent coefficient/coast evidence fits the predeclared decision budget. Unsupported powered base or roughness assumptions stay explicit uncertainty scenarios; do not fit them to apogee. Supplied drag tables must carry axis, incidence, Re/environment, power-state and source provenance, not just two arrays called Cd.

## 4. Atmosphere/wind: a practical improvement requiring inputs rather than a new aero theory

**Current code.** `six_dof.rs:921–949` uses fixed standard sea-level temperature/pressure, tropospheric and isothermal branches, a 0.14 power-law wind speed profile, constant wind direction and zero vertical wind. The local wind comment says “toward” while the function adds π. The inspected UI explicitly says “Wind from” (`src/console/stages/Flight.tsx:208`), and design/adapter forward its azimuth. That boundary agrees with the actual conversion; correct/document the comment and test the full path, rather than flipping physical wind by 180 degrees. Standard atmosphere is a reference distribution, not the weather of a launch. [NOAA definition](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=standard+atmosphere).

**Replacement.** One immutable `EnvironmentProfile` supplies pressure, temperature, wind ENU and derived density/sound speed/viscosity to trajectory, aero, calibration and analysis. Record geographic/vertical datum, observation or forecast issue time, units, interpolation and extrapolation. Support measured pad pressure/temperature plus a declared fallback lapse profile first, then archived forecast/sounding profiles; use meteorological wind-from only at an explicit import boundary. Preserve standard atmosphere as an identified fallback.

**Experiment/acceptance.** Analytic hydrostatic/isothermal fixtures, standard-profile regression, layer continuity, ground-to-air velocity and wind-from cardinal cases establish implementation. Measured pad and aloft profiles establish environmental applicability; launch-time forecast and retrospective reanalysis are separate evidence classes. Repeat the same development flight inputs under independently sourced profile alternatives and attribute rho/speed/aero changes. Acceptance is the decision's allocated atmosphere uncertainty, with spatial/time sampling limitations. If aloft wind is missing, surface power law can produce scenarios but not a calibrated landing-containment probability.

## 5. Mass properties and damping: needed for weathercocking/rail/stability, not inferred from apogee

**Current code.** `six_dof.rs:1370–1399` models the entire dry vehicle as a uniform cylinder at its rollup CG, adds a uniform motor at a fixed centroid and imposes equal transverse inertias. Motor mass follows cumulative impulse (`:1103–1129`), not a measured grain mass/centroid history. Pitch/yaw damping use the same fixed 1.5 multiplier and roll terms use fixed 4.0 (`:1513–1535`). Correct tensor-axis mapping does not make these mass distributions or derivatives accurate.

**Replacement.** Carry a measured or component-derived dry inertia tensor and CG, motor case/propellant properties and the provenance of their evolution. Start with the declared axisymmetric class the diagonal kernel can support; either bound products of inertia or separately authorize a full-tensor kernel change. Do not discard significant cross terms silently. Replace damping with geometry/rate-dependent derivatives in the same coefficient provider after normal-force semantics are fixed; preserve reduced-rate definitions and source domains. Independently review variable-mass angular-momentum/outflow assumptions before treating `I_dot` alone as a full rocket attitude model.

**Bounded experiment.** Weigh the assembled flight article and balance launch/burnout-equivalent configurations; compare component sums/parallel-axis identities to these measurements. Pendulum tests of a known calibration body then the inert vehicle can constrain inertia with a repeatability and fixture-error budget. A rocket-stage methodology specifically studies bifilar roll and compound-pendulum transverse measurement and identifies suspension/amplitude/frequency errors. [Possamai, TU Delft 2023, inspected abstract](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:5e1373e2-cb76-49cd-9779-e9823009c5bd). Controlled rate tests or qualified attitude-rate flight data are needed for damping; calibrating inertia and damping simultaneously from apogee cannot identify them.

**Acceptance/fallback.** Analytic constant-mass torque-free/angular-momentum and known-torque cases qualify implementation, not the variable-mass physical model. Compare observed attitude/rate histories only with known mass properties, sensor alignment and atmosphere, and separate static restoring slope from damping. If these data are absent, keep dynamic stability/coning and weathercocking precision unqualified even when static CP is useful.

## 6. Recovery: steady descent is the feasible first accuracy claim

**Current code.** `models.rs:542–559` assigns parachutes by order, reusing one canopy if only one exists. `six_dof.rs:1413–1434` switches immediately to a constant canopy Cd and circular area, replaces airframe drag, and zeroes the static aero arm under recovery. This does not describe inflation, wake interactions, independent sections or opening loads. Better integration cannot supply the missing physical parameters.

**Replacement/experiment.** Represent explicit drogue/main roles, connected recovery mass/configuration, deployment trigger and delay, steady effective `CdA`, and an optional measured inflation history. Begin with drop/flight intervals after completed inflation, measured system mass, density, vertical/air-relative speed and time; estimate effective CdA with uncertainty and test it on another interval/flight. In still-air terminal descent the diagnostic identity is `CdA=2mg/(ρv²)`; use the full acceleration balance when terminal conditions are not established. Keep each separately descending section explicit.

Deployment, inflation and steady descent are different physical phases; useful opening-load prediction needs configuration-specific filling/drag-area and load evidence. [NASA SP-8066, §§2.1 and 4.6, inspected](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710021827/downloads/19710021827.pdf). A fitted CdA or an arbitrary area ramp cannot validate harness shock. Acceptance for descent/landing uses the team's speed/energy and terrain/wind reserve. Fallback is steady-descent scenarios with unqualified opening loads and containment; ground ejection success alone does not close either.

## 7. Production schema and execution order

Implement a versioned provider interface, shared by curves, local diagnostics, flight and reported stability; do not maintain independent production CP laws.

```
AeroModelSpec: modelId/version; component methods/equation citations;
  geometryHash; source/dataHash; legacy/calibrated/supplied provenance;
  reference { S_m2, L_m, D_m, datum_B_m, axisConvention, angle/rate definitions };
  domains { geometry, Mach, Reynolds, alpha/beta, rates, power/base conditions };
  interpolation/extrapolation; assumption IDs; qualification evidence IDs

AeroState: relativeVelocity_B; alpha/beta; Mach; rho/T/mu;
  rate_B; instantaneousCG_B; powered/nozzle condition; environmentIdentity

AeroEvaluation: totalForceCoeff_B; staticMomentCoeff_datum_B;
  dampingMomentCoeff_B; component breakdown; totalWindCoefficients;
  smallAngleDerivatives { dF/dalpha, dF/dbeta, dM/dalpha, dM/dbeta };
  optionalCP { value, derivative-versus-finite-force definition, conditioning };
  modelDomainStatus; empiricalQualification { quantity, conditions, evidence };
  assumption/uncertainty IDs; numerical/support diagnostics
```

Keep these supporting records separate: `MassPropertiesProvider` (mass/CG/tensor and derivatives, motor source), `EnvironmentProfile`, `RecoverySystem` (roles/sections/CdA/events), and `CalibrationArtifact` (estimator and selector versions, raw-support identity, conditions, fitted regime and *application* regime). The current scalar calibration applies to powered and other airframe regimes; CAL-E2 selection repair does not authorize that extrapolation. Add an explicit scoped correction provider for the qualified coast regime, and label legacy global application by its existing semantics. Do not automatically copy its multiplier to the new aero model.

**Practical computation/update architecture.** Precompute immutable geometry integrals (wetted areas, fin strips, reference transforms, component moment integrals) once per geometry/model identity. Interpolate environment at every actual load evaluation, including adaptive stages and event/root probes; derive local airspeed/Mach/Re from that state. Direct evaluation of surface friction is inexpensive and avoids a new full multidimensional table. Cache Mach-only pressure/base terms where their method truly has no other dependence. A supplied table declares only the axes it resolves: e.g. Mach and log(Re), with separate power/base conditions and optional incidence/roll. Store nodes, coefficient conventions, interpolation and coverage explicitly. Sparse data must not masquerade as a dense resolved Mach×Re×alpha×roll grid. Interpolation error is checked against direct/source nodes and propagated to outputs before choosing refinement. Roughness or geometry edits invalidate the appropriate cache; environment, model or calibration edits invalidate prediction identity. Curves query the same provider at declared conditions; no separate curve defaults become flight inputs. Event-conditioned power state and atmosphere must not be frozen at display-telemetry cadence.

**Implementation provenance.** Independently implement the mathematical method from published equations and primary theory, with equation-to-function traceability and source licensing review. Do not copy GPL OpenRocket engine code, redistribute restrictive reference material, or restore removed closed/vendor components into this Apache project. The public method document is evidence of its author's model, not permission to transplant implementation. Source adaptations, fitted constants and missing derivations require separate review; vendor cross-code outputs alone cannot become a source-derived native model.

## 8. Feasible measurement path versus CFD/tunnel expenditure

The lowest-resource useful path is **measured mass/CG and inertia, explicit guide/recovery geometry, local weather plus wind aloft, existing certified COTS thrust data with uncertainty, a coherent published engineering aero model, and independently instrumented full-scale flights**. Existing four development flights can diagnose estimators and record regressions; their reconstructed mass/weather and limited channels cannot uniquely identify force, moment, inertia, wind and thrust simultaneously. A flight-restart diagnostic at a qualified observed burnout state would isolate coast prediction from launch/burnout mismatch; it still needs clock/velocity/attitude uncertainty and must never be scored as a launch prediction.

Use matched public coefficient evidence first. If the chosen mission's decision reserve remains sensitive to transonic moment/drag, run a **bounded geometry-specific experiment**: a low-speed balance test can test signs and low-incidence slopes but cannot qualify transonic shock behavior; a suitable transonic/supersonic tunnel needs Mach/Re/transition/roll/cant, base/sting corrections and simultaneous force/moment measurement. Select its state grid from the actual flight envelope and disputed branch behavior before outcomes. New measured data enter a declared development or sealed validation role before access.

CFD is a targeted candidate-data source when the required tunnel or flight inversion is impractical: compute the same total force and datum moment for actual geometry at declared Mach/Re/incidence and power state; quantify mesh/domain, convergence, turbulence/transition and geometry sensitivities; compare the workflow against already exposed relevant measurements before using new predictions. A steady inviscid calculation cannot establish viscous/base drag; cold-flow RANS cannot establish a powered plume; steady results do not identify dynamic damping or parachute inflation. CFD-assisted tables require discrepancy and applicability evidence just as engineering formulas do. No cost or schedule is asserted without facility/tool access and a defined experiment.

The practical release objective is a primary simulator for a **declared collegiate COTS mission family and specified decisions**, including qualified supplied aero where necessary. An analytic-only universal M0–4/30-degree accuracy promise is not supported. The path closes definitions immediately, acquires inexpensive high-value inputs first, spends specialized aerodynamic effort only on unresolved decision-sensitive regimes, and finally tests predictions prospectively.
