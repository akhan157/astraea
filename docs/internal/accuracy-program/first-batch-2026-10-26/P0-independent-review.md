## Findings resolved in the final reviewed candidate

**R1 — make the velocity-reference wind assumption explicit (§1).** The rigid-body relation quoted there is exact for inertial point velocities. Applied to the declared air-relative velocities at P and G, it additionally assumes the wind is spatially uniform between those points at the evaluation time. In one common vector basis the general relation is:

```
v_air(P) = v_air(G) + omega × (r_P − r_G)
                         − [wind(P) − wind(G)]
```

The owner corrected §1 to distinguish ground-relative from air-relative velocity, state the spatially uniform wind assumption, and require subtraction of the wind difference in a common frame. The reviewer verified that wording. Coordinates must share a basis and evaluation time; rotate navigation-frame wind differences into B when using B quantities. Translating a moment datum does not supply this conversion. No new shear model is required by this correction. **Resolved.**

**R2 — identify the deliberately omitted fin CP movement (§4).** Printed p27 says that the quarter-MAC CP starts moving aft above approximately Mach 0.5, and describes interpolation between Mach 0.5 and 2. The candidate freezes quarter-MAC for its entire `0 <= Mach < 0.8` evaluation support. The owner corrected §4 to identify fixed quarter-MAC over approximately `0.5 < Mach < 0.8` as a deliberate SB1 simplification and discrepancy to measure, rather than a complete transcription of the source CP method. The reviewer verified that wording. The source's approximate subsonic boundary at M0.8 (Table 3.1, printed p18) does not establish that fixed quarter-MAC is accurate throughout that interval. Narrowing support would be another defensible design choice, but is not required to accept this openly provisional specification. **Resolved.**

## Mathematical and source findings

**Frames and exact force transformation.** The ENU navigation frame, body +Y forward, paired alpha/beta definitions, and `omega_B=(q,p,r)` agree with the baseline normative contract. The stated wind basis has its second axis along `v_air_B/V`, is orthonormal and right handed, and becomes the identity at zero angles. Transposition gives the three declared body-to-wind coefficients, including the side-force sign. At beta=0 the drag and lift reductions are correct. The power identity follows directly from `F dot v = V F dot eYw`; multiplying by qS gives the declared dimensional expression. The full force must enter that identity. The CA/CN totals and explicit CD0 distinction prevent a second addition of wind drag.

**Moment datum, CP and restoring signs.** `M_G=M_O+(r_O-r_G) cross F` and its coefficient version have the correct sign. With nose datum and centerline CG this gives `Mx/(qS)=(J-xG C) alpha` and `Mz/(qS)=-(J-xG C) beta`. An arbitrary full datum vector is supported by the general cross product. Positive pitch rate decreases positive alpha at the symmetric reference state; positive yaw rate increases beta under these axes. Thus K>0 supplies the stated restoring signs in both planes. The derivative-CP formulas include the datum station and the yaw sign correctly. A vanishing total C with nonzero J retains a moment and has no finite derivative CP. Negative C and cancellation must not be removed by a positivity guard. The document appropriately separates diagnostic CP existence/conditioning from stability and dynamic frequency.

**Body equations and symbol map.** Technical-document (3.18), (3.24), and (3.25), printed pp22–23, retain sine terms at finite incidence. Their zero-angle slopes, with `Aref -> S` and source moment length `d -> L`, are exactly the candidate's C and J/L. Extending the local interval to global stations gives `J=2[x1 A(x1)-x0 A(x0)-integral A dx]/S` by integration by parts. The x0 term is necessary. This verifies the cone result, zero cylinder contribution, and the possibility of a nonzero moment with equal endpoint areas. It does not qualify a finite-angle sine law or the accuracy of numerical quadrature.

**Fin equations and symbol map.** Printed p26 defines Afin as the one-sided area of one fin and s as its root-to-tip span. For a trapezoid, source `Xt -> m`, `Cr -> cr`, `Ct -> ct`, and adding `xLE` converts the local station to the nose datum. The candidate's station is algebraically (3.34): `(cr+ct)-cr*ct/(cr+ct) = (cr^2+cr*ct+ct^2)/(cr+ct)`. Its cosGamma uses the midpoint-to-midpoint sweep. Equation (3.40), printed p29, has numerator `2*pi*s^2/Aref`, not `2*pi*(s/D)^2`; its compressibility beta is `sqrt(1-M^2)` and is distinct from sideslip. The candidate transcribes this correctly. The N/2 multiplier follows from (3.53); the extra fin-count interference multiplier of (3.54) is one for the candidate's single 3/4-fin set. Equations (3.55)–(3.56), printed p34, supply exactly `1+r_t/(s+r_t)` and explicitly ignore the smaller induced force on the body. These are internally consistent with the candidate's chosen model and its stated omissions.

**Rates and scaling.** Reduced-rate length/factor, row/column order, velocity point, and rate-minus-zero-rate definition are explicit; no universal dissipativity is presumed. Changing reference S by k and L by h rescales a moment coefficient by `1/(k*h)`, but its derivative with respect to a correspondingly redefined reduced rate by `1/(k*h*h)`. The inspected diagnostic checks this dimensional invariance for one scalar rate-moment example. It does not test a real derivative matrix or provider.

**Support, statuses and migration.** The initial Mach and five-degree limits are explicitly computational candidate policy, with no empirical tolerance inferred. Undefined zero-speed angles/rates, supported zero dimensional static loads, unsupported forward-flow/geometry cases, optional missing derivatives, and computational versus empirical status are distinguishable. A1 must map these meanings to the existing user-facing validity statuses during implementation; the specification is not proof of that mapping. Legacy identity, save/reopen reproducibility, explicit migration, stale calibration, and cache invalidation are appropriate design requirements. They remain unimplemented and untested in this review.

## Limits and follow-up ownership

The 5,214 assertions cover the wind basis, transforms/power, wrench translation, selected cancellation/body/fin fixtures, and one reduced-rate reference rescaling. They do **not** cover all eight fixture groups in §7, statuses, serialized migration, actual production force assembly, numerical integration of arbitrary geometry, Rust/WASM implementation, or measured aerodynamics. Counting these assertions is not an acceptance tolerance or a validation percentage.

The source itself warns on printed p23 that body lift can matter at only a few degrees, and discusses roll-angle effects on printed p33. Their omission is model discrepancy; the small angle cap alone does not prove insignificance. Likewise the inherited zero-incidence axial provider and any legacy rate couple remain separately identified and unqualified for the new composite. This review makes no new assessment of their accuracy.

The source prefactor and candidate normalization are verified. This review does not establish that an isolated prefactor change repairs legacy trajectories: the existing model's diameters, fin count, and separate interference/body contributions must be evaluated together before making that claim. The candidate's explicit prohibition on such a conclusion is retained. A complete independent reconstruction of the original Barrowman thesis, source licensing/legal review, and a new audit of legacy engine implementation were outside this focused review.
