# What we need from a rocketry team

**Purpose.** One page a team lead or mentor can act on. Deliberately vendor-neutral: no team should
have to fly a specific brand or own specific hardware to help. Item 1 alone is the big one.

---

## The short list

**1. One flight, as files**
- your flight computer's log, exported however your software exports it (CSV, or a track file)
- which motor was flown (designation; the curve file if you have one)
- the vehicle's dimensions and mass — or your design file from whatever tool you use

**2. A few more flights like #1** (different vehicles, motors, or conditions) — turns one comparison
into a real error picture.

**3. The motors you actually fly**, and for reloadable or experimental motors the grain shape you
use (BATES, star, moon, C-slot, finocyl, D, X-core, rod-and-tube, or something else).

**4. Your recovery practice** for real builds: bay dimensions, packed parachute volume and weight,
what density you consider too tight, shear-pin size, and the black-powder charge you used.

**5. Where a simulation disagreed with a real flight** — with the numbers if you have them. That
tells us which model to fix next instead of guessing.

**6. 30–60 minutes** walking us through how one of your designs moves between your tools today:
which tool for which decision, where files get re-entered by hand, what you re-check before launch.

**7. What a post-flight report must contain** for you to actually use it (for a mentor, a sponsor,
or a design review).

**8. What new members get confused about** most often.

**9. Two or three people** willing to run our desktop checklist on a Windows machine and report
what breaks.

## Data handling

- We ask before using anything, and say plainly how it will be used.
- Only derived comparisons get published by default (predicted vs measured). Raw logs, vehicle
  names, and team names stay private unless you say otherwise.
- Anything you send stays yours; credit is optional.

## What we do **not** need

- New hardware or a new flight — any past flight works.
- Money or a formal sponsorship.
- Certification authority or endorsement from anyone.
- Files from commercial tools you don't want to share.

## If you happen to have these (optional, never required)

- A raw flight-computer flash image **together with** that same flight's exported log and the
  firmware version — both halves are needed, since a raw image alone can't be validated.
- A staged or clustered flight's details: separation timing, tip-off notes, configuration.
- A policy answer from your range safety officer: would a computed validation package count as
  supporting data, and what would a witnessed validation flight require?

## Ready-to-send message

> We're building Astraea, an open-source rocket engineering workstation (airframe, aero, propulsion,
> 6-DOF flight, and flight-evidence in one place). To check it against real flights and build the
> parts that actually matter, could you share:
>
> 1. One past flight: your logger's exported log, which motor flew, and the vehicle's dimensions and
>    mass (or your design file).
> 2. The motors you fly most, and the grain shapes you use for reloadables.
> 3. Your real recovery numbers: bay dimensions, packed chute volume/weight, shear pins, charge used.
> 4. 30–60 minutes on how a design moves between your tools today.
>
> No new flights or hardware needed — any past flight is enough. We'll publish only derived
> comparisons and nothing that identifies your team unless you say so.
